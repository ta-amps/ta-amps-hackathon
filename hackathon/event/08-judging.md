# Judging

Submissions are scored on four weighted criteria. The weights come from the Official Rules.

| Criterion | Weight | What judges look at, in our words |
|---|---|---|
| **Impact** | 30% | Real-world potential: who benefits, how much it matters, whether people could actually use it, and whether it fits one of the [problem statements](05-problem-statements.md) |
| **Use of Meta AI Tooling** | 25% | How creatively you used Meta's tools, whether you went "beyond a basic integration", and whether you surfaced capabilities nobody expected |
| **Technical Depth & Execution** | 20% | Sound, refined engineering that shows real craft |
| **Demo** | 25% | A working, impressive demo that "holds up live", and the quality of the demo video |

## Track emphasis

The event page adds a lens per track: Indie projects are judged on "creativity, technical ambition, and whether it
works"; Startup projects on "technical execution and whether it holds up in production". See [03-tracks.md](03-tracks.md).

## Stages

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Nov 4 to Nov 8 | Judging. A panel of judges selected by the organizers picks the finalists. |
| Nov 9 | Closing ceremony with live finals; winners announced. |
| about Nov 10 | Bonus awards announced (categories may include a community vote and most viral project). |

The rules don't publish the finals format. Plan for a short live run that works even if the network doesn't.

## Our reading (opinion, not official)

- Two criteria (Demo 25% plus part of Impact) reward something that works in front of people. A recorded fallback and
  an offline mode protect half the score.
- "Beyond a basic integration" suggests several Meta tools doing distinct jobs, not one model call.
